# Networking (`src/net`)

ADRs 0007 and 0013 are the contract.

- At most 32 `RTCPeerConnection`s per browser, counting boundary neighbours, witnesses and handoffs: P0 (fight and witness duty) and P1 (creatures that can interact) up to 32, P2–P4 only in the room under 24 they leave. Never evict P0 or P1 while P0 + P1 ≤ 32; past it keep P0 and the nearest P1 and say `crowded` (ADR 0062).
- Never hide an opponent who can still affect the player: every creature within interaction range and ±1 tier is connected, or shares no instance with the player.
- Every inbound message is untrusted: check its version, cap its size (snapshots are fixed-size; events ≤ 2 KB) and validate it before use. Malformed input is dropped and counted, never thrown into the game loop.
- A peer is authoritative only over its own creature's free-roam movement. Changes to other creatures or to lineages go through claims, the authority or a referee.
- Bandwidth: about 10 Hz snapshots of ≤ 32 bytes per peer (version 5 is 29 bytes: x and y as u32 in 0.125 wu steps; a signed frame is 93 bytes), reduced by distance and tier. Keep it under about 4 KB/s per connection.
- No per-frame or per-snapshot authority traffic. The authority sees joins, signaling, critical transitions and batched commitments (budget in ADR 0008).
- Tests use fake transports, or real WebRTC between local browser contexts through the local Worker. Never public STUN, TURN, relays or the production authority.
- `src/net/protocol.ts` re-exports `protocol-defs.ts` (types, constants, snapshot codec) and `protocol-parse.ts` (untrusted-JSON parsers); all are shared with `worker/`, so keep them free of DOM and Node APIs.
- Proxy frames (`peer-mesh.ts` `onProxy`, `proxy-snaps.ts`): a 61-byte frame carries an away fighter's pose, relayed by every connected fighter to its own linked peers outside the fight (M11 T16). It is never the sender's own pose, and the receiver drops it (`net.proxyDropped`) when the sender's own snapshot isn't fighting, when that player sent its own snapshot in the last second, when another sender of that player sent within the last second (one sender per away player, the first heard; a closed link frees it at once), or past 3 away players per peer. A proxied fighter is drawn faded and never offered for an attack.
- Witness messages (protocol 10, ADR 0058) parse in `protocol-witness.ts`, which refuses unknown fields at every level it defines; records, commitments (a `witness` commitment's owner is `''`) and beacons parse in `protocol-docs.ts`. Caps: a `batch` holds 1–20 frames of 1–4 slots, a signature exactly beside each non-null input, within `BATCH_MAX_BYTES` (12 KB); a `duty` is within `DUTY_MAX_BYTES` (128 KB), its pool position below the pool's size, its commitment's event and round its own, and a referee duty's history (frames from 0 in order, at most 900, and up to 2 joins) carries no `enc`; `finput`, `wframe` and `probe` stay within `EVENT_MAX_BYTES`; `lock-state.seen` holds at most 5 snapshots: the attacker's that the target received in the last 2 s (`SignedSnaps.recent`, which keeps each peer's last 5 verified frames), for the movement check at lock-in (M10 T11, ADR 0059); `lock-ask.by` names the attacker. The authority meters `batch` and `dispute` in their own window (30 per 10 s, dropped and counted in `Authority.dropped` past it) for a fighter or a referee only; every other witness message counts in the 20-message budget.
- A fight's wire (`encounter-sync.ts`): the referee's `enc-start` repeats for a resync (after a reconnect or an authority restart) with the history, which replaces the client's own; an input for a finalized frame is `late` and never changes a bundle. Tune the lead's constants against `docs/perf/netcode.md` (`npm run report:net`), not by feel.
- A witness referee (ADR 0058, M10 T3): `handover.ts` holds the spans (`RefereeSpans`, the authority from 0, each later span strictly after the last) and the handover frames (`h = next + 3` to a witness and for a planned take-back from the frame due now, `max(next, due) + 2` after a failure). `witness-referee.ts` is the witness's side, pure: the same `Referee` restored at `h` with the fight's joins, its own copy of the encounter stepped from the duty's history and the authority's frames up to `h − 1` (it finalizes nothing before they are in), a signed `wframe` per final frame, a `batch` every 10 frames and a final one (`end`) at the fight's end or a stop; it drops an `finput` from a non-fighter or another fight (`stray`), before `h` (`late`), past a stop (`moved`) or badly signed (`forged`), so it never batches an input the authority would refuse. `referee-route.ts` is the fighter's: each frame's input to that frame's referee (signed to a witness), the lead from that referee's latency, a resend of the inputs already sent from a handover's frame on, the witness's last 50 signed frames, and on an authority frame that differs, `EncounterSync.overrule` and one `dispute` per witness. Neither touches sockets or WebRTC; the route takes its signer and frame check injected, so it loads no `@noble/curves`. A page passes `tickOnInput: false` and finalizes in a task of its own (M10 T7, `src/game/app-witness.ts`), so an input's check and a frame's step never share one handler; `enc-referee` reaches a page as a fight message (`FightMsg`).
- Duty links (ADR 0058, M10 T6): a priority-0 link between a referee witness and its fight's fighters, anywhere in the world. `ConnectionPolicy.duty(id, on)` makes the peer P0, so duty links take the total past the default cap of 24, never past `MAX_CAP` (32), and each takes the place of the worst P2–P4 peer while one is held. In the page (M11 T13), `OnlineSession.dutyPeer(enc, peer, on)` (a hub `duty-peer`, or the witness's own duty) hands it to the plan (`Links.duty` in `src/game/online-links.ts`), which dials it at once through the hub like any P0; `{ on: false }`, `duty-end` and `enc-end` take its class away, and the plan closes it 3 s later unless another class holds it. The hub relays a signal anywhere between a witness and the fighters of the fight whose referee duty it holds, offered or taken (`hub.duties.linksOf`), and otherwise only between sessions its directory places within reach (M11 T14).
- The world's messages (protocol 11, M11 T2, ADR 0062) parse in `protocol-world.ts`, which refuses unknown fields at every level it defines; seat tickets parse in `src/ledger/seat-doc.ts` (`parseSignedSeat`, no key checks; the checks live in `seat.ts`, which loads `@noble/curves`). `HubClient.ticket` keeps the welcome's ticket and each fresh `seat`'s. The welcome names `world` (`WorldInfo`) and may carry `ticket`, and holds no roster: there is no `members` and no `member` message (M11 T14); a hub `signal` may carry `at`, the sender's directory position. Ranges: positions in 0 to 2^29 wu (exclusive), radii 1–4096 wu, a `where`'s `aoi` 1–40,000 wu; a `near` holds at most 48 distinct peers (`NEAR_MAX`) and 64 swarm points (`SWARM_MAX`) within `NEAR_MAX_BYTES` (8 KB, the hub drops the farthest to fit); a gossip `peers` at most 16 distinct ids (`GOSSIP_MAX`); a `relay` alone may pass `EVENT_MAX_BYTES`, up to `RELAY_MAX_BYTES` (10 KB) with its `sdp` within `SDP_MAX_BYTES`, `at` a whole ms and `cls` 0–4; a signal's (hub or relay) `kind` is `offer`, `answer` or `refuse` (M11 T13: the callee's refusal, with an empty `sdp`), and a hub `signal` may carry `cls`, the link class its sender holds the receiver in (`parsePeerEvent` and `PeerMesh.sendEvent` apply the cap by type); `rate` is 1, 2, 5 or 10 Hz. `claim` and `consumed` slot ids may carry a food layer `t1`–`t4` before the cell (`t2c4_7_s3`); any other id starting with `t` is refused. The authority meters `where` and `gone` in the 20-message budget.
- Spawning, riding and crowd instances (protocol 12, M12 T1, ADR 0064) parse in `protocol-world.ts` too, refusing unknown fields at every level they define. An instance is a whole number from 0 (the open water) to `INSTANCE_WIRE_MAX` (65,535); `sameWater(a, b)` in `instance.ts` (with `CROWD_AT`, `CROWD_EVERY_MS` and `INSTANCE_MAX`) says whether two instances share water. A `where` may carry `crowd`: 1 to `CROWD_LIST_MAX` (32) distinct player ids. A client `spawn` carries `r` (1–4096) and `aoi` (1–40,000) and a `ride` nothing; a hub `spawn` carries `x` and `y` on the wire, `why` (`placed`, `back`, `ride`) and a boolean `protect`, `ride-no` one of `locked`, `busy`, `company`, `soon` or `none`, and `instance` its `n`. Every `near` peer carries `n`, a peer `hello` its sender's `n`, and every gossip entry a fifth element, its instance; a peer `instance` event tells a change. The welcome's `you` is optional. The new refusals (`engage:instance`, `spawn`) travel as `refused` reasons. Until the tasks that use them land, `spawn` (M12 T4) and `ride` (M12 T7) are answered, and both count in the 20-message budget. The page routes a hub `spawn` and `ride-no` as a `LifeMsg` (`src/game/spawn-ask.ts`, `src/game/ride.ts`; a ride's `spawn` settles no spawn ask). `WhereReporter.nudge()` asks for a `where` as soon as the 2 s gap allows: `OnlineSession` nudges when the page turns alone (the hub times a ride's wait from that report) and on `reportSoon()` (a ride's arrival). It names its own instance in its hello (`HelloGate.instance`; `PeerMesh.instanceOf(id)` reads a joined peer's) and each gossip entry's by the freshest word of it, and the hub client hands `instance` to the world listeners (`WorldMsg`).
- Crowd instances in the pure page code (M12 T3, ADR 0064): `Known.n` (the instance its source gave) and `AoiSelf.n` are optional, and none means the open water. `classify` gives no class but P0 to a creature that doesn't share water with the player, by its newest sighting's instance, so a fight's opponent in another instance stays P0. `WhereReporter.update(state, leaf, crowd)` takes the page's crowd list, or null while it isn't crowded: turning crowded reports at once (within the 2 s gap) unless a crowd went in the last `CROWD_EVERY_MS` (10 s), then every 10 s while crowded, and every `where` while crowded carries the list. `crowdList` builds it from the linked peers' snapshots: within 8 × the larger radius, `linkTiers`, sharing water, nearest first, at most 32; with none, the sender's own id alone, which says it is crowded and pairs it with no one. Gossip entries carry their instance: `gossipFor` leaves out what doesn't share water with the receiver (by its fix's `n`), and `Gossip.receive(via, event, now, own)` drops an entry from water `own` doesn't share.
- Crowd instances in the page (M12 T6, ADR 0064): `src/game/crowd-view.ts` (`CrowdView`, owned by `OnlineSession` as `crowd`) keeps the page's own instance (the hub's `instance`; a welcome starts again at 0) and each creature's freshest word (the directory's `near`, a link's hello and `instance` events), which `Neighbours` stamps on its snapshot sightings and on any older sighting. On a change of its own instance the page sends every link an `instance` event first, asks the directory again (`WhereReporter.nudge`: its held `near` peers carry the water of before; M12 T8), then plans: a held link that no longer shares water closes at once unless it is P0 (`Links` with `shares`), and an offer from other water is refused unless either side holds it as P0. A linked peer is drawn while it shares water or fights beside the page; one that isn't gets no snapshot or game event (`broadcastState`, `broadcastEvent`) and only its duty events (`finput`, `probe`, `wframe`) are taken from it, and `OnlineHandlers.water(id, false)` starts its 1 s fade (`RemoteSet.fadeOut`, which keeps it past its link's close until the fade ends; `fadeIn` or a new link ends it). A link the other side closes within 3 s of the page's own change fades too, as its `instance` event may not have come. The page is crowded while its P0 + P1 that share water reach `crowdAt` (`CROWD_AT`, `debug.crowdAt` 2–24), and its `where` then carries `crowdList`. `OnlineHandlers.busy` shows the busy toast on leaving the open water, at most once a minute; proxies of an away fighter go only to spectators that share water with it (`RelayHost.sees`, `OnlineSession.sharesWith`).
- Links by area of interest (M11 T8, ADR 0062), pure: `aoi.ts`'s `AoiManager.classify` turns the known creatures (snapshot, gossip, directory, signal; the newest of each id) into classes, the first that holds: P0 the ids passed in; P1 within `INTERACT_RADII` (8, the engage candidates' `CANDIDATE_RADII`) × the larger radius + 600 wu, held to + 900; P2 within the AoI (`aoiRadius`: 2.65 × `viewHalfHeight`), held to 1.1 ×; P3 within 2 ×, held to 2.2 ×; P4 within `queryRadius` (max(8,192, 4 × AoI), the directory's query radius too), held to 1.1 ×, ordered from where the player will be in 3 s. A hold applies to a creature whose previous class was that one or better; every class but P0 needs `linkTiers`, and P1 is symmetric. `AoiManager.plan` and `ConnectionPolicy` share `selectLinks` (the cap rule above); a held peer with no class keeps only leftover room under 24, for `UNWANTED_GRACE_MS` (3 s). Either side may call, so the mesh settles glare (perfect negotiation); `admit` judges an inbound offer by the caller's class against the links held now. `where.ts`'s `WhereReporter` says when to report: a `where` on entering the water, leaving the hub's leaf by more than 64 wu (unless the leaf doesn't hold the last report yet: the answer is on its way), a tier change, an AoI change past a quarter, or 30 s; a `gone` once on leaving the water; never two within 2 s (a trigger waits); values clamped to the wire's ranges.
- Gossip, relayed signaling and the hello gate (M11 T9, ADR 0062), pure but for the mesh's gate. `gossip.ts`: `Gossip.due` gives each link a `peers` event at once and then every 4 s (`gossipFor`: the sender's up to 16 linked peers nearest the receiver's newest snapshot, `linkTiers` with it, from snapshots under 2 s old, positions rounded to whole wu, so 16 entries stay within 1,100 bytes); `receive` keeps each entry as a `gossip` sighting for 10 s (at most 48 per sender, never itself or the sender), `heard(id)` says which linked peers placed it. `signal-route.ts`: `SignalRoute.send` is the mesh's `signal`: an offer goes through the linked peer nearest the target among those whose gossip placed it, else the hub, and `tick` resends it once through the hub after 4 s unanswered; an answer goes back the way its offer came. A peer forwards a relay only from its own sender (`from` is the link it came on: one hop) to a linked `to`, unchanged; every link passes at most 4 relays a second, in or through (`limited`). Every signal carries `cls`, inside the signature, and a P0 or P1 offer goes through the hub (`send(…, viaHub)`); a `refuse` goes back as an answer does and cancels the resend. An inbound relay must name this page, carry an `at` within 30 s of the hub clock and a (from, at) not taken in the last 2 minutes, and pass `signalSigned` last (`forged`; only a genuine one is remembered, so a forgery cannot burn a real pair). The signer, the check and the transports are injected, so the route loads no `@noble/curves`. `PeerMesh` with a `hello` gate sends its ticket as each link's first event and joins a peer (`onPeerJoined`, `isOpen`, `ticketOf`) only once that peer's first event is a `hello` whose ticket passes the injected check (`seatValid` for that peer); until then its snapshots are dropped, and a wrong first event, a failing ticket or none within `HELLO_MS` (5 s) closes the link with no retry (`helloFailed`). A later `hello`, or one on an ungated mesh, is dropped. A caller takes one answer per offer and ignores a second copy (a late relayed one beside the hub's after a fallback). `rtc-test-kit.ts` holds the fake `RTCPeerConnection` the unit tests share.
- The headless harness (M11 T10, ADR 0063): `tests/aoi-harness.ts` runs simulated clients (the real `AoiManager`, `WhereReporter`, `Gossip`, `SignalRoute`) against the real `Directory` over a seeded link model, with scenarios and the eleven gates in `tests/aoi-scenarios.ts`; `npm run report:aoi` writes `docs/perf/aoi.md`. Since M12 T8 the harness also runs the hub's `Crowds` (a crowded client's `where` carries its crowd list; each moved member hears its instance after the hub's latency; a link that no longer shares water closes at once unless P0), enters clients by `place()` (`ClientSpec.place`) and can ride a lone client (`Scenario.ride`, off in every shipped scenario); `Scenario.instances: false` runs a scenario without crowds, for comparison. Gate 10 (crowds) and 11 (spawns) are judged in `gatesOf`; a fight's second fighter is picked at its start as an engage picks (`FightSpec.engage`). The population wave (M12 T9, ADR 0066: `tests/wave.ts`, `npm run sim:wave`, `docs/perf/wave.md`) judges the same rules at 300 players against eight gates of its own, with `ClientSpec.leaveAt` and `Scenario.wave`. Change a link rule against its gates, not by feel. `classify` puts a creature in P2 to P4 by whichever of the two creatures sees further. The harness models the page's dial loop with the page's own constants (`src/game/online-links.ts`).
- The page's links (M11 T13, ADR 0063): `src/game/online.ts` (`OnlineSession`: the hub, the mesh, `where` and `gone` by `WhereReporter`, gossip every 4 s, signals through `SignalRoute`), `neighbours.ts` (the known set: `near`, linked peers' snapshots, gossip, signal positions; a snapshot or signal places a creature for 10 s) and `online-links.ts` (`Links`, the plan: every 500 ms and at once on a `near` or an offer; P0 and P1 at once through the hub; the rest through a gossip-placed peer, else one hub call at a time once 4 links are held, a creature younger than 8 s dialling freely, at most 8 a plan; the lower id calls, the higher waits 1.5 s; a P4 only on a sighting under 5 s old; an offer carries its caller's `cls` and is admitted by the better of that and the callee's own class, against the links held; a refusal is answered with a `refuse` signal and backs off a P3 or P4 5 s, doubling to 60 s; an offer unanswered after 8 s is dropped). The mesh runs with `retry: false`, so only the plan calls, and answers a repeated offer again instead of rebuilding the link. The page calls and admits only once it holds a ticket and has loaded the seat checks and the authority's keys (the checks load `@noble/curves`, imported lazily). A fight's other fighters are P0 from its `enc-start` (a joiner's catch-up included) or a known fight's `enc-join` until `FIGHT_HOLD_MS` (3 s) after its `enc-end`, across leaves and tiers, and then close at once unless another class holds them; a welcome starts that hold for every fight, and the hub's resync of a running one cancels it (M11 T16). `OnlineSession.protectedLinks` counts the P0 and P1 wanted, and a page offers itself as a witness only at 28 or fewer (`AVAIL_MAX_LINKS`). There is no roster (M11 T14): names come from tickets and the directory, the status line counts the linked peers, and a fighter is away while this page holds no open link to it (`present`). Every linked peer's traffic reaches the game.
- Snapshot rates by distance (M11 T15, ADR 0062): `snapshot-rates.ts`, pure. The receiver asks (`wantedRate`): 10 Hz for P0, P1 or within 1.15 × its view's 16:9 half-diagonal, 5 Hz for P2 or within its AoI, 2 Hz for P3, 1 Hz for P4 or a held peer with no class; `RateAsker` sends a `rate` event on a change, at most once a second per link, and `OnlineSession` asks every tick from the newest snapshot's distance (10 Hz with no creature). The sender's `SendRates` picks each link's share of the 10 Hz frames by the snapshot number `SnapshotSender` passes (a rise takes the next frame); a candidate's signed frame goes on every signed frame whatever its rate. Both ends forget a link's rate when it opens or closes, so a new link runs at 10 Hz until asked. `RemoteBuffer.sampleLate` draws a remote its snapshots' spacing + 20 ms behind (the smallest of the last 3 spacings, 120 ms at 10 Hz, at most 1,020), shrinking the lag at 0.2 ms a ms and growing it at 0.5, so a rising rate eases in at 1.2× at most and nothing jumps; `pace` keeps the fastest it was drawn (a creature moved past `SNAP_DISTANCE` is drawn there at once and is no pace). The harness's gate 9 counts each frame on the wire with 93 bytes of headers (`FRAME_OVERHEAD_BYTES`): at 24 links at most 12 KB/s (not asserted in the dense scenarios, where every link is P1), one link at most 4 KB/s.
- Relay reports and the line's reason (protocol 13, M13 T4, ADR 0067): a `progress` may carry `relay` (`RelayReport`: exactly `links` 0–32, `relayed` 0–`links`, `kb` 0–`RELAY_KB_MAX` (16,384), whole), and `queued` carries `why` (`QueueWhy`: `full`, `budget` or `storage`); the parsers refuse a missing, extra or out-of-range field. `relay-stats.ts` is pure: `selectedPair` finds a link's selected candidate pair (the transport's `selectedCandidatePairId`, else the nominated pair that succeeded) and whether its local candidate is `relay`; `RelayStats.read` counts each read's links, relayed links and the bytes received over relay pairs since the last read (per pair, so a switch counts from the new pair's start and a counter that went back restarted), and `take` gives the totals since the last report (the most links and relayed of any read, the KB with the bytes under a whole KB carried). `PeerMesh.linkStats()` reads each joined link's `getStats()` and the last stats of each link closed since (asked before its `close`, at most 64 kept). `OnlineSession` reads every `RELAY_READ_MS` (10 s) while it holds links (and once after the last closes), and `LifeFlow.report` takes the totals (`LifeDeps.relay`). Reports are untrusted: the authority clamps them (`worker/lives.ts`).
