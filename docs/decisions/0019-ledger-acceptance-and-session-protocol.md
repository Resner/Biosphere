# 0019: Record acceptance envelope, ledger traffic and genesis policy (M4)

- Status: accepted (amends 0009 where it says `t` is a record field)
- Date: 2026-10-26

## Context

ADR 0009 says the owner signs a record's hash and the authority countersigns, with `t` set by the authority. Both cannot hold if `t` is inside the hashed record: the owner would have to sign a time they cannot know. M4 also has to fit the ledger into the existing hub socket, within ADR 0008's budgets (about 20 incoming messages per player-minute, at most 10 SQLite row writes per player-hour), keep a creature's condition from being rolled back by closing a tab, and decide how hatching works while the authority or the beacon is unavailable. DESIGN §5 limits genesis to one per 10 minutes, which would make dying early in a session a long wait.

## Decision

- **Acceptance envelope.** A *record* is what the owner signs: `{ v, rules, kind, creature, seq, prev, owner, body }` (canonical JSON, integers and strings only). `hash = SHA-256("bs/rec/v1\n" ‖ JCS(record))`. `prev` is the hash of the creature's previous record, or `null` for a genesis. On acceptance the authority stores an *acceptance* `{ hash, t, age, key, sig }`: `t` is its clock in ms, `age` the creature's active time in ms after this record, and `sig` is its Ed25519 signature over `"bs/acc/v1\n" ‖ hash ‖ t ‖ age ‖ key` (each field as fixed-length bytes or decimal text, specified in `src/ledger/record.ts`). The creature's id is the hash of its genesis record, and the next record's `prev` is the previous hash, so history is a hash chain where every link has an owner signature and an authority acceptance. Verifiers (M9) check both.
- **Transport.** Ledger traffic uses the existing hub WebSocket, with its single session per player. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` becomes 2; a version 1 client sees "Update available". New client messages: `genesis` (request a hatch), `tx` (a signed record), `pulse` (a signed checkpoint proposal). New hub messages: `genesis-wait`, `genesis-ready` (carries the beacon), `ack`, `reject`, `expired`, `queued`; the welcome gains `you.creature` (the resumable head, its genome, condition, age and lifespan) and `you.genesis` (a pending commitment). Every message stays within `HUB_MAX_BYTES` and goes through the shared codecs.
- **Genesis flow.** The client asks to hatch. The authority checks the limits, commits `round = latest verified + 2` and answers `genesis-wait` with the commitment id and round. When the round exists it fetches and verifies the beacon, stores it, and sends `genesis-ready` with the beacon. The client verifies the BLS signature, recomputes `genesisGenome(seed)` itself (seed from `SHA-256("bs/genesis/v1\n" ‖ randomness ‖ commitment id ‖ slot 0)`), builds the genesis record `{ kind: 'genesis', seq: 0, prev: null, body: { commit, round, genome } }`, signs and submits it with `tx`. The authority recomputes the same genome, compares, and only then accepts. A genesis whose genome differs from the recomputation is rejected and flagged. The commitment is bound to the player id; resending the request returns the same commitment (no reroll, no second round).
- **Genesis limit.** DESIGN §5's "1 per 10 minutes" becomes a token bucket: burst 5, one token back every 5 minutes. A player with an active creature cannot start another. The bucket keeps farming expensive but lets a new player who starves three times in a row hatch again at once. The numbers live in `RULES.ledger` and are the owner's call at a playtest.
- **Unregistered life.** When the authority is unreachable, a player is queued, rate limited, or a beacon wait fails, the game still lets them swim: a local random genesis body (the M3 behaviour), marked "unregistered" in the HUD. It writes nothing to the ledger, cannot mate (M5) and never resumes. This replaces the old always-local hatch, which stays as the offline fallback only.
- **Active time.** The authority measures a creature's active time as the time its owner's session is open and the creature is the active one, from `welcome` to socket close. It is persisted with each accepted record and on close, never per tick. Lifespan is `75 min × the lifespan trait` as integer milliseconds computed by shared code (`lifespanMs(genome)`). At 85% the sim applies the old-age modifiers (thrust −20%, basal +20%); at 100% the authority sends `expired`. The client answers with its signed death record (cause `old-age`) within 15 s, otherwise the authority writes an authority-only death record flagged `forced`. Offline time never counts: nothing runs when no session is open.
- **Pulses and checkpoints.** Every 15 s the client sends `pulse`: a checkpoint proposal (`kind: 'checkpoint'`, `prev` = head, `seq` = head.seq + 1, `body` = condition in hundredths) signed by the owner. The authority keeps only the newest pulse per session, in the socket attachment (no SQLite write), and commits it as the creature's next record when the session closes, on a death or rehatch, and at least every 20 minutes of active time. Because it commits the owner's own signed proposal, a closed tab saves the condition of at most 15 s ago, which makes closing a tab a poor way to escape a dying body.
- **Plausibility.** A committed checkpoint is checked against the previous head: biomass cannot exceed `previous × exp(growth rate × active seconds)` or the genome's maximum, energy and health cannot exceed their maxima, and the creature's age is the authority's. Values outside are accepted as signed but flagged (`flags` table), and the creature's *effective* condition (what a resume restores, kept on its `creatures` row) is the clamped one. Records are never rewritten, so a verifier recomputes the clamp from the chain. Flags feed M10.
- **Beacons.** The authority verifies and caches every beacon it uses (`beacons` table). Quicknet's BLS12-381 verification uses `@noble/curves`, pinned to the chain's public key. A fixture mode (`DRAND_SOURCE=fixture`, local development and tests only) serves recorded beacons and maps the clock onto them (wrapping when the recording ends). `ACTIVE_TIME_SCALE`, an environment variable honoured only in fixture mode, speeds up active time so tests can reach old age.
- **Admission.** A usage meter counts incoming messages and estimated row writes per UTC day, persisted every 5 minutes. When concurrent players reach `MAX_PLAYERS` (default 400) or the day's usage passes 90% of its budget, new sessions are queued with a position and an estimated wait and admitted in order as slots free. A queued socket may still play an unregistered life.
- **Recovery code.** `exportRecoveryCode()` and `importRecoveryCode()` live in `src/ledger/identity.ts`, so the seed still never leaves that file except through the code the player asked for. Importing stores the new seed, and the server's single-session rule closes the old session.

## Consequences

- Records stay owner-signed and replayable while the authority's time and age claims are separately signed, so a verifier can distinguish what the player attested from what the authority witnessed.
- Per-player authority load outside genesis is about 4 pulses a minute (well under 20 messages) and a handful of row writes an hour; genesis costs a few rows once per hatch.
- A drand outage delays hatching only; the player can swim unregistered meanwhile.
- A hard crash within 15 s of the last pulse loses that window's growth, never its age.
- Rejected:
  - putting `t` and `age` into the owner-signed record (the owner cannot know them);
  - a second round trip per checkpoint to fetch `t` before signing (doubles traffic for nothing);
  - the authority writing checkpoint records on the owner's behalf (breaks "every record is owner-signed");
  - separate HTTP endpoints for transitions (a second session notion, extra auth);
  - per-tick or per-second condition reporting (blows the write budget).
