# 0071: What one authority object serves, the session caps, and what 10,000 players need

- Status: accepted, amended by 0075 (the free plan caps at 80% of one object, and a headroom cap follows the day's allowances) and by 0079 (the model prices matings and promotions: 88.0 rows written a player-hour, not 65.5) (fills in ADR 0067's capacity rule from the measurements: amends 0067 by setting the session caps and the paid plan's allowances; confirms ADR 0056's 14-day log window; settles ADR 0008's question about splitting hubs: not yet)
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Context

ADR 0067 fixed how M13 measures: three layers of evidence, a capacity rule for one object (CPU at most half a core with the local runtime's measurement doubled, at most 1,000 incoming messages a second, at most 64 MB of hub state), and the ceiling of $9.50 a month. `docs/CAPACITY.md` is generated by `npm run report:capacity` from `docs/perf/*.json` and `worker/plan.ts` (`worker/capacity.ts` is the model, `tools/capacity-report.ts` the report). The numbers below are its current output; the file is the source.

## Decision

**One object serves 777 concurrent players.** CPU binds. A player sends 10.8 messages a minute outside fights and about 19.6 with fights (3 an hour, half witnessed), and the real process spends 0.99 ms of CPU per message, doubled for production: 777 players use half a core. The message rate (3,065) and the hub's own state (77,942 sessions at 861 B) don't bind. The state is measured as what the hub keeps (an attachment and a directory entry), not the process's resident memory, which also holds the runtime, SQLite's cache and the garbage of 500 hatches.

**The session caps** are the smaller of the plan's money cap and 80% of the capacity (621):
- the **paid plan** serves **590** concurrent players around the clock within $9.50: it caps at 590, and its daily allowances are 916,289 request-equivalents, 1,666,666 rows written and 833,333,333 rows read, which spend $4.44 above the base at the measured mix (the limit is $4.50);
- the **free plan** serves 1,526 player-hours a day, 63 concurrent players around the clock, and caps at **63**. Rows written run out first (65.5 an hour, 55.5 of them in fights), then request-equivalents (64.7 an hour).

`FREE.sessions`, `PAID.sessions` and the paid allowances come from `derive()` in `worker/capacity.ts` over `MEASURED` in `worker/plan.ts`; a test fails when `MEASURED` differs from what the committed JSON files give.

**10,000 concurrent players need 17 objects** (shards of 621 at the 80% cap) and cost about $1,391 a month on the paid plan: $70 of requests, $65 of duration (each object awake all day at 128 MB), $422 of rows written (15.7 M a day) and $805 of TURN at a 15% relay share. 3,262 messages a second in all, 38% of a core each. The €10 ceiling does not buy them, and nothing here builds them: admission's cap keeps the live world inside one object.

If shards were built, the design is the directory's own: the world's square splits by area into objects, each seating the players whose creature is in its part and handing over at the border; the ledger and the clock stay in one object, and the shards message it for records. The cost of that one more hop is not measured. **Sharding stays unbuilt** because one object serves 777 ≥ 200 (ADR 0067's rule).

**The log window stays 14 days.** The free plan at its full daily allowance keeps the logs for 14 days (214 MB) and grows the history by 8.4 MB a day, so its 1 GB store lasts 94 days, over the 90 the rule asks. The numbers are column bytes; SQLite's pages and indexes add some, which the `storage` guard (90% of the plan's store) covers. `LOG_KEEP_DAYS` is unchanged, and `docs/perf/storage.md` now takes its limit from the plan (1 GB, not ADR 0056's 5 GB). A test fails when the rule says 7.

**The levers**, weighed in `docs/CAPACITY.md`, are not pulled:
- witnesses refereeing every fight lift one object to 1,142 players but not the free plan, which rows written bind;
- relay for P0 and P1 links only cuts TURN by two thirds and moves no limit the model binds;
- the newcomer's dial and the lone creature's report change a joiner's first minute, not the hold;
- a checkpoint every 30 minutes saves 3% of rows written;
- checkpoint compaction and archiving history are built only if a store limit demands it: the history grows 5.5 KB a player-hour.

**Assumptions that need the playtest**: 3 fights an hour, 50% witnessed, a relay share of 15% and 30-minute sessions. M14's `/v1/metrics` replaces them through `npm run report:capacity -- --metrics <file>`; if one object's capacity falls below 200 there, the hottest path gets a task before shards.

## Consequences

- The free plan's session cap falls from 200 to 63. It can be raised with `MAX_SESSIONS` when the owner accepts the day's budget guard ending the day sooner.
- `docs/CAPACITY.md` is generated and never edited by hand; a change to a measurement means regenerating it and `MEASURED`.
- Rows written in fights (37 per duel, mostly the log's batches of 10 frames) are the free plan's first limit. A larger batch would lift it and is the cheapest lever if the free plan matters; it changes the referee's persistence and is left to a later milestone.
- **Rejected**: building shards ahead of a measured need; taking the process's resident memory as the object's state; shortening the log window with a store that lasts 94 days.
