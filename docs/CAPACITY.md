# Capacity and cost

Generated by `npm run report:capacity` from `docs/perf/*.json` and `worker/plan.ts` (M13 T8, ADR 0067 and the capacity ADR). Never edit by hand.

## Summary

- **The free plan** serves **1,526 player-hours a day**, about **63 concurrent players** around the clock. It stops at 100,000 request-equivalents or 100,000 rows written a day (rows written first); admission queues newcomers at 90% of either and until 00:00 UTC. Its session cap is **621** (80% of one object), and the headroom seats a newcomer only while everyone seated could play 2 more hours of the day: 763 seats on a fresh day, so the cap binds, and a crowd of 200 plays 5.6 hours of a fresh day before the headroom stops newcomers.
- **The paid plan** ($5 a month plus usage) serves **590 concurrent players** around the clock within **$9.50** (€10 at $0.95 to the euro): $9.44 a month, $4.44 of it above the base, all of it requests.
- **One authority object** serves **777 concurrent players** (cpu binds: 50.0% of a core at 2× the local runtime's CPU). Admission fills 590 sessions on the paid plan and 621 on the free plan.
- **10,000 concurrent players** need **17 objects** (shards of 621), 3,262 incoming messages a second and 15.5 M request-equivalents a day, and cost about **$1,390.86 a month** on the paid plan ($69.74 requests, $65.50 duration, $421.66 rows written). The ceiling does not buy them.

## Operating limits

- **One object**: 777 concurrent players. By CPU 777 (50.0% of a core, the local runtime's 0.99 ms a message doubled), by messages 3,065 (1,000 a second), by memory 77,942 (64 MiB, a limit this model does not bind). The smallest binds: cpu.
- **Session caps**: 621 on the free plan (80% of one object, ADR 0075), 590 on the paid plan (the smaller of what $9.50 serves, 590, and 80% of one object, 621). On both, the headroom (below) seats fewer late in a busy day.

| Plan | Requests a day | Rows written a day | Rows read a day | Storage | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | 100,000 | 100,000 | 5,000,000 | 1.0 GB | 621 |
| Paid | 916,289 | 1,666,666 | 833,333,333 | 10.0 GB | 590 |

The paid allowances are each meter's included amount spread over 30 days, or what 590 players use a day if that is more; at the measured mix the month costs $9.44, $4.44 above the base (the limit is $4.50). Admission queues at 90% of them.

### How long the store lasts

At the full daily allowance of each plan (the free plan's 1,526 player-hours, the paid plan's 14,160), with the 14-day log window.

| Plan | Store | Days to full |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 1.0 GB | 94 |
| Paid | 10.0 GB | 103 |

The rule (ADR 0067): the free plan keeps the 14-day window if its store lasts 90 days at its full allowance, else 7. At 14 days it lasts 94, so the window is **14 days**. The figures are column bytes; SQLite's pages and indexes add some, which the `storage` guard (a store at 90% stops new sessions) covers.

### Crowds from a fresh day

The headroom (ADR 0075): a newcomer is seated only while everyone who holds a seat, plus it, could play 2 more hours within what is left of the day's allowances (to 100%) at the per-player-hour costs above. A crowd that stays at its size from 00:00 UTC has its freed seats refilled until the headroom falls below it, 2 hours before it would spend the first allowance; the budget guard stops newcomers once 90% of it is spent. The day's allowances reset at 00:00 UTC, so a crowd that reaches neither within 24 hours plays all day.

## The admission policy, as built

- A newcomer waits when one of three guards holds: `full` (seated sessions and away fighters reach the smaller of the session cap and the headroom), `budget` (the day's request-equivalents, rows written or rows read reach 90% of the plan's allowance) or `storage` (the database reaches 90% of the plan's store).
- Its wait is measured: for `full` the time to the next seat plus the mean interval between the last 20 seats freed for each session ahead, and while the headroom binds one more interval for each seat held past it; for `budget` the time to 00:00 UTC plus that interval for each session ahead; for `storage` none.
- A seated player is never dropped, and a player whose seat closed within 2 minutes is seated again past any guard. The TURN guard stops the relay for newcomers before the month's allowance runs out.

## Beyond one object

10,000 concurrent players need **17 objects** at the 80% cap, each serving about 588: 3,262 incoming messages a second in all, 37.8% of a core each. Nothing in this repository shards the world; the design, if it is ever built, is in the capacity ADR. The month costs about $1,390.86, of which duration is $65.50 (each object awake all day at 128 MB), requests $69.74 and rows written $421.66. A relay at 15.0% of traffic is 17,107 GB a month against 1,000 included.

## Levers

In the order to pull them. Each shows its effect on the numbers above.

| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| More fights refereed by witnesses | At 100% (now 50.0%): a fight costs 100 messages against 350; one object serves 1,142 (now 777) and the free plan 1,526 player-hours a day (now 1,526). |
| Relay only for the closest links | A third of the relay (5.0%) cuts TURN to 0.79 MB a player-hour (now 2.38); it moves no limit the model binds. |
| The newcomer's dial and the lone creature's report (ADR 0066) | Both cut `signal` and `where` traffic for a joiner's first minute; the model's steady hold does not change, so neither is pulled. |
| The log window | 7 days instead of 14 saves 106.8 MB of the free plan's store at its full allowance; the rule above decides it. |
| Checkpoint every 30 minutes | Rows written an hour fall by 2.0, 3.1% of the total; no limit moves. |
| Checkpoint compaction, or archiving history | The history grows 5.5 KB a player-hour for good; at the free plan's full allowance that is 8.4 MB a day. Built only if a store limit demands it. |
| The paid plan | $5 a month plus usage serves 590 concurrent players within $9.50, against 63 around the clock on the free plan. |

## Reproducing

`npm run report:capacity` regenerates this file from `docs/perf/load.json`, `hub.json`, `hub-load.json`, `storage.json` and `witness.json` and `worker/plan.ts`; the regeneration is byte for byte. `npm run report:capacity -- --metrics <file>` takes a saved `GET /v1/metrics` answer from a playtest and replaces the messages, requests, rows and relay traffic per player-hour with what it measured. A playtest of 200 should land within a factor of two of the table above.
